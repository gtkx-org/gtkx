# Gold Standard Report — packages/native (architecture lens)

## Summary

Reviewed ~8,160 source lines of `packages/native` — the Rust napi addon that drives GTK4/libadwaita from JavaScript through runtime `Type` descriptors rather than compile-time bindings — measured against gtk-rs/gtk4-rs and gtk-rs-core/glib 0.22. The addon is a faithful and in several places better-fitted dynamic analogue of glib's compile-time marshalling and subclassing machinery: its `Descriptor`→`Codec` seam, `enum_dispatch` codecs, toggle-ref wrapper lifetime, and two-mailbox thread model are correct adaptations of the deliberate single-GLib-thread, one-descriptor constraints, not workarounds. The headline conclusion is that the architecture is sound and most divergences from gtk4-rs are justified by those constraints; the single HIGH finding is a missing `panic::catch_unwind` at the two GTK→Rust `extern "C"` trampolines, which turns any recoverable codec panic into a full GUI-process abort, and the remaining confirmed findings are contained module-layering, DRY, lifetime, and dead-code cleanups.

## Reference projects

- **gtk-rs-core / glib 0.22** (`~/.cargo/registry/.../glib-0.22.7`) — studied for the `translate`/`Value` marshalling model (`ToGlibPtr`/`IntoGlibPtr` vs `FromGlibPtrNone`/`Full`/`Borrow`, `Stash`/`Storage`, `FromGlibContainer`, `TransparentPtrType`), the object/toggle-ref system (`ObjectRef` `Clone`/`Drop`, `add_weak_ref_notify`, `watch_closure`), the `MainContext` cross-thread scheduler (`invoke`/`invoke_with_priority`, `invoke_local` owner gate), the `ThreadGuard`/thread-affinity discipline, `glib::subclass::types` (`register_type`, `TypeData::parent_class`, `g_type_add_interface_static`), and its module DAG (`object`→`value`→`translate`, with `error`/`log` as sibling leaves).
- **gtk-rs / gtk4-rs** (`~/gtk4-rs`) — studied for the per-widget module layer sitting atop generated `auto/` (`gtk4/src/lib.rs`), the typed vfunc-slot install pattern and `parent_*` chain-up helpers (`gtk4/src/subclass/widget.rs`), and the `gir` GIR-codegen `manual`/`ignore` contract (`Gir.toml`) that keeps generated surfaces free of fields their consumers ignore.

## What packages/native already does well (do not break)

**Layering and seams**

- `request` is a genuinely clean top layer: nothing in `ffi`/`handle`/`messaging` imports `crate::request`, each `request/*.rs` owns exactly one `#[napi]` export depending only downward, and the uniform `Request`/`Response` trait pair (`request.rs:24-76`) gives every export the identical GLib-thread-hop + JS-conversion shape — the dynamic analogue of gtk4's per-widget layer over `auto`.
- The `Descriptor` codegen contract crosses at exactly one seam: `Descriptor::into_codec` lowering, consumed only at the request boundary (`bind.rs:32`, `read.rs`, `write.rs`, `register_class.rs:82`) and depending only downward on `ffi::codec`. This mirrors glib's single declared marshalling seam and is kept just as narrow.
- The two-layer wire/internal split mirrors gtk-rs's sys/safe layering: the camelCase `Descriptor` wire vocabulary is lowered exactly once by `into_codec` into the internal `Codec` at every JS→native entry, and only the `Codec` is retained.
- The `codec` sub-tree layers cleanly through one shared-helper leaf (`super::prelude` plus targeted `numeric`/`string` forwarding) with no sibling web, and `enum_dispatch` over a closed `Codec` enum (`codec.rs:230-251`) gives static, vtable-free dispatch — the right call over `Box<dyn>` given the variant set is fixed by codegen.

**Marshalling model**

- The `Encoder`/`Decoder`/`PtrWriter` triad (`ffi/codec.rs:101-249`) is the correct dynamic collapse of glib's compile-time directional trait split (`ToGlibPtr`/`IntoGlibPtr` vs `FromGlibPtrNone`/`Full`/`Borrow`), branching on a runtime `Ownership` field because the transfer annotation arrives per-call in the `Descriptor`; the default `encode`/`read` hooks (`ref_for_transfer`/`transfer_release`/`object_ptr_context` and `ReadSource::{Call,Slot,Value}`) let all four pointer codecs share one ownership path without per-codec duplication.
- `StashedValue` is correctly NOT a re-implementation of `glib::Value` — it is a lightweight libffi-argument carrier, avoiding a heap `GValue` + `GType` lookup per argument. `Stash`/`StashStorage` is the necessary runtime-tagged form of `translate::Stash`/`Storage`, with a `PendingTransfer`/disarm rollback that glib (eager `IntoGlibPtr`) has no analogue for.
- The A/B collection boundary is drawn correctly: native reuses `glib::StrV`/`ByteArray` exactly where the element type is statically expressible and hand-rolls strided/pointer walks only for scalar and GObject-pointer arrays where glib's `Slice<T>`/`PtrSlice<T>` would need a compile-time `TransparentType`; the three-way container transfer distinction is modeled compositionally (`array.ownership` + `item.ownership`) rather than as an extra enum variant, and borrowed scalar arrays are backed by a plain `Vec` to avoid a needless GLib-allocator round-trip.
- `ArrayKind`/`ListOps` runtime fn-pointer dispatch tables faithfully substitute for glib's compile-time-generic collection wrappers while preserving transfer-mode-per-element semantics.

**Subclassing and callbacks**

- Manual `g_type_register_static` + `g_type_class_ref` + direct byte-offset vtable writes (`register_class.rs:250-278`) are the correct, unavoidable dynamic analogue of gtk4's typed `klass.snapshot = Some(widget_snapshot::<T>)`, since the type name, parent, and vfunc set all arrive from JS at runtime — offsets supplied by `@gtkx/codegen` from GIR instead of by the Rust type system. Reusing the parent's queried class/instance sizes and installing no instance-private storage is the right simplification given all subclass state lives in JS keyed by the wrapper.
- One `CallbackState`/`closure_entry` trampoline backs both the vfunc path (leaked via `mem::forget`, matching a vtable slot's process lifetime) and the signal/callback path (scope-managed via `CallbackScope`), with the ownership-lifetime difference expressed cleanly rather than by duplicating the libffi `Cif`/`Closure` construction. Interface-method overrides via `g_type_interface_peek` on the freshly-ref'd class struct are the correct GObject primitive.

**Object lifetime and threading**

- The toggle-ref + strong/weak-flip design (`handle/wrapper.rs`) is necessary and correct: glib keeps a wrapper alive with one plain strong ref because a gtk-rs wrapper *is* the strong ref, whereas a JS wrapper must stay alive iff C still holds a ref — the gjs/pygobject language-binding idiom, not a reinvention. The generation counter + `LOOKUP_LOCK` guard a genuine cross-thread read-clone-vs-steal race that gtk-rs never faces.
- `GlibThreadState` (`library_cache.rs`) correctly quarantines every non-`Send` GLib-thread resource behind one thread-local, matching glib's thread-affinity discipline so the Node thread structurally cannot touch GTK state. The two-mailbox + freeze architecture does not merely reinvent `MainContext::invoke` — it provides synchronous cross-thread RPC, depth-filtered draining, and the React-commit freeze loop that `invoke`'s opaque idle dispatch cannot express — while cross-thread result handoff via `std::sync::mpsc` mirrors glib's own `ThreadPool` idiom. `Handle::drop`/`AnchoredValue` route every owned free onto the GLib thread via `is_owner()` + `idle_add_once`.

**Codegen contract**

- Native embeds zero per-type or per-symbol GIR knowledge — every `type_name`, `shared_library`, `symbol`, and ref/unref/free fn comes off the wire — leaving the `@manual` override escape hatch one layer up in `@gtkx/gi`, exactly how gtk-rs keeps `translate.rs` generic. The authoring vocabulary (`t.*`) is correctly larger than the wire vocabulary, and every one of the eight `ArrayKind` strings the runtime emits is reachable in both encode and decode. The `Request`/`Response` split keeps `type Output: Send` free of any JS-conversion bound, so `execute()` stays testable off the Node env.

## Findings

### Pattern Alignment

**[HIGH] GTK→Rust callback trampolines run codec decode/encode with no `panic::catch_unwind`, so a single codec panic aborts the whole GUI process**
- **Where:** `ffi/callback.rs:311-321` (`closure_entry`, `unsafe extern "C"`, no catch) → `handle_call:178-222` → `read_args:143-176` / `write_return:224-238`; `handle/wrapper.rs:185-213` (`on_toggle_notify` on-owner-thread branch); the two existing catches sit above the C main-loop frame (`glib_mailbox.rs:132`, `node_mailbox.rs:77`) and cannot intercept.
- **Standard:** glib catches *at* the `FnOnce` a C frame invokes wherever it has a routing sink — `main_context_futures.rs:262` `panic::catch_unwind(panic::AssertUnwindSafe(|| Pin::new(&mut self.future).poll(&mut context)))` routed through `tx`, and `thread_pool.rs:90` `let _ = tx.send(panic::catch_unwind(panic::AssertUnwindSafe(func)))`. Under the crate's default `panic=unwind` (no `panic=abort` profile; edition 2024), unwinding out of a plain `extern "C"` (not `extern "C-unwind"`) function is a defined process abort.
- **Why it matters:** every other addon boundary contains a panic and routes it to `ErrorReporter` (surfacing a Node `unhandledRejection`) while keeping the GLib loop alive — the reason `panic_handler` + `error_reporter` exist. The trampolines are the one class that instead aborts. Any codec panic in `read_args`/`write_return` (debug arithmetic overflow, a malformed `from_glib` pointer, allocation failure, the `unreachable!` at `stashed_value.rs:212`) becomes a full-app crash, and moving the catch one frame up cannot help because the panic must first cross `g_signal_emit`/`g_main_context_dispatch` C frames.
- **Suggested change:** wrap the bodies of `closure_entry`, `on_toggle_notify` (on-thread branch), and the `wrapper.rs:155` idle cleanup closure in `catch_unwind(AssertUnwindSafe(..))`; on `Err`, route the payload to `ErrorReporter` (native already has this sink) and write a null/default return via the return codec.
- **Constraint fit:** fully survives — the catch lives inside `closure_entry` on the GLib thread, so no GTK work moves off-thread and the mailbox handoff is untouched, and it is orthogonal to dynamic `Descriptor` dispatch and the codegen seam. Honest grounding: glib's *plain* trampolines (`main_context` invoke, `source.rs` `trampoline_local`) do not catch either, so the standard is the narrower "glib catches-and-routes precisely where a routing target exists" — which native has at exactly these two trampolines, making the omission an internal-consistency defect. The specific `prelude.rs:79` `.expect` trigger is only weakly reachable; the defect is the general absence of any guard at these boundaries.

**[MEDIUM] Two codec sites divert per-call failures to the async `unhandledRejection` sink, defeating the caller's synchronous try/catch**
- **Where:** `ffi/codec/enum_flags.rs:34-59` (`validate_enum_value` reports async, then `encode` returns the encoded value regardless); `ffi/codec/boxed.rs:19-29` (`gtype()` reports async, returns `None`) with sync-path callers at `:77-93`/`:97-122`/`:125-153`. Sync entry points `call.rs:37-41`/`:78-80` lower `anyhow::Error` → `napi::Error` at `request.rs:36-45`; ~52 sibling codec `bail!`/`ensure!` sites propagate synchronously.
- **Standard:** glib surfaces an operation failure as a *returned* value — `error.rs:15` `pub struct Error(Boxed<ffi::GError>)`, branched via `matches`/`kind`/`domain`/`code`/`message` — and reserves its only process-global channel (`log_set_writer_func`, which native already mirrors as `GlibLogHandler`) for logging. Native has both channels; these two sites pick the log-style sink for a return-style failure.
- **Why it matters:** on an invalid enum, `validate_enum_value` fires a global `unhandledRejection` *and* still passes the invalid value to GTK, so the caller's try/catch sees success; on a gtype-resolution failure `boxed` silently degrades (no `PendingRelease` → ownership leak, `from_glib_full` with `None`, raw-pointer write) and returns spurious success with an uncorrelated async rejection later. The two codecs even disagree on the identical `resolve_gtype` failure (boxed reports it, enum_flags silently skips validation).
- **Suggested change:** make `boxed`'s `gtype()` return `anyhow::Result<Option<Type>>` (or have callers `bail!`), and change `validate_enum_value` to `anyhow::Result` with `encode` `?`-propagating it — matching the `numeric.rs:212-231` range checks — so both failures reach the JS caller synchronously.
- **Constraint fit:** fully survives — reuses the existing `anyhow::Result` signatures, detects on the GLib thread, and returns through the existing `invoke_glib_and_wait_napi` result channel (already used for panics); no static per-type error enum, no `Descriptor`-seam change. The vfunc-return path (`write_return_to_ptr`) legitimately keeps `ErrorReporter`, since there it has no synchronous frame to return through.

**[MEDIUM] `ffi`↔`handle` is a bidirectional module cycle where glib's equivalent marshalling layer is a strict one-way DAG**
- **Where:** back-edges `handle`→`ffi`: `handle/boxed.rs:7` (imports `ffi::codec::BoxedFreeFn`), `:100` (calls `ffi::dup_to_glib_heap`), `handle.rs:158/164/170` (three `From<handle::…>` for `ffi::value::Value`). The 11+ forward edges `ffi`→`handle` are the correct direction.
- **Standard:** glib keeps `object`→`value`→`translate` strictly one-way — `object.rs:17/:33` depend on `value`, but `value.rs` has zero references to `object` and `translate.rs` zero to either (grep-verified). The marshalling seam never reaches back up.
- **Why it matters:** `ffi` and `handle` cannot be reasoned about or unit-tested as independent layers, and the `Boxed` concept is physically split — the ownership struct in `handle/boxed.rs`, its free-fn signature type `BoxedFreeFn` in `ffi/codec/boxed.rs:70` — so its invariant lives in two modules. All five back-edges are trivially relocatable, so the cycle is accidental rather than essential.
- **Suggested change:** move the `BoxedFreeFn` type alias into `handle/boxed.rs` (which already imports `handle::Boxed`), move the three `From` impls into `ffi/value.rs` (which already imports `handle::Handle`), and give `dup_to_glib_heap` (a one-line `g_memdup2` wrapper) a small leaf home.
- **Constraint fit:** fully survives — pure module reorganization orthogonal to dynamic `Descriptor` dispatch, the single GLib thread, and the codegen seam; the one-way-DAG property is independent of static-vs-dynamic binding. Drop the finding's recompile-cascade rationale: intra-crate cycles are legal in Rust and carry no incremental-recompile penalty, so the genuine cost is contained to reasoning/testability/cohesion within one crate.

**[LOW] The Node inbox (`NodeTask`) names domain-owned payload types, inverting dependency direction versus glib's opaque `MainContext::invoke`**
- **Where:** `messaging.rs:29-30` / `node_mailbox.rs:12-13` import `ffi::value::{JsRef,Value}` and `handle::wrapper::WrapperRefOp`; `NodeTask` variants `messaging.rs:68-77`; `WrapperRefOp` def `handle/wrapper.rs:12-35`. Contrast the already-generic GLib inbox `GlibTask = Box<dyn FnOnce() + Send>` (`messaging.rs:35`).
- **Standard:** glib's cross-thread scheduler transports an opaque boxed `FnOnce` and names no caller type — `invoke_with_priority` boxes into `trampoline::<F>` (`main_context.rs:57`, `:120-130`), capturing typed state at the call site.
- **Why it matters:** because `NodeTask` enumerates tasks naming `Value`/`JsRef`/`WrapperRefOp`, the transport depends *up* into `ffi` and `handle`, forming the `handle`↔`messaging` and `ffi`↔`messaging` cycles. `WrapperRefOp` is especially inverted: a pure Node-thread napi-ref op (messaging's own domain, `apply()` only calls `napi_reference_ref`/`unref` on a `usize`) is defined in `handle` and re-imported by `messaging`.
- **Suggested change:** relocate `WrapperRefOp` into `messaging` — it is messaging's *only* `handle` import, so this severs `messaging`→`handle` and collapses that cycle; it mirrors the structurally identical `JsRefDeletion`, which already lives in `messaging`.
- **Constraint fit:** the `WrapperRefOp` relocation survives all constraints (napi-domain op, not a `Descriptor`, not GTK work). Narrowed honestly: co-locating `JsRef` does *not* break `ffi`↔`messaging` because `NodeCallback` still ferries `Vec<Value>`, and fully genericizing `NodeTask` to `Box<dyn FnOnce>` like the GLib inbox is not adoptable — the Node inbox carries dynamic `Value` payloads and participates in depth-tagged reentrancy that glib's compile-time-captured closures have no equivalent for.

### Subclassing & callbacks

**[MEDIUM] `class_ptr` is discarded after slot writes, so a JS vfunc cannot chain up to the parent implementation it overrides**
- **Where:** `request/register_class.rs:271-278` (`g_type_class_ref` result used only to write slots, then dropped), `install_into:137-152` (`slot.write` overwrites the parent fn pointer without reading it); no parent-invocation path in `ffi/callback.rs` `handle_call:157-198`. A repo-wide grep for `peek_parent`/`parent_class`/`chain` over `native/src` is empty.
- **Standard:** glib treats chain-up as core subclass machinery — `TypeData` caches `parent_class: ffi::gpointer` (`subclass/types.rs:442`, set in `class_init` at `:946` via `g_type_class_peek_parent`), documented "used for chaining up to the parent class' implementation of virtual methods" (`:472-482`); gtk4's `WidgetImplExt::parent_compute_expand` reads `parent_class() as *mut GtkWidgetClass` then calls `(*parent_class).compute_expand(...)` (`gtk4/src/subclass/widget.rs:240-257`).
- **Why it matters:** GLib memcpy's the parent class struct into the derived struct before `class_init`, so the pointer `install_into` overwrites at each `byte_offset` is the parent's own implementation — after the write it is unreachable from native. Full replacement is fine for leaf drawing widgets (`ColorWidget.snapshot` in `frames.tsx:56` repaints the whole surface), but any subclass that must *extend* rather than replace (draw a background then let the parent render children, augment `measure`/`size_allocate`, wrap a `GtkListItemFactory` step and still call super) is inexpressible.
- **Suggested change:** read the existing slot with `slot.read()` before `slot.write()` and stash it in `CallbackData` (or record `g_type_class_peek_parent(class_ptr)` once per class); surface a "call parent vfunc" path that reuses the vfunc's existing arg/return `Cif` to invoke the stashed implementation, mirroring gtk4's `parent_*` helpers.
- **Constraint fit:** constraint-compatible — this is a missing capability, not a duplication of compile-time machinery; the parent pointer is pure runtime data needing no concrete Rust `ParentType` or monomorphized trampoline, and the parent call runs on the GLib thread inside `handle_call`, riding the depth-tagged reentrancy already present. No current gtkx subclass needs chain-up (ColorWidget fully repaints; the data-holder GObjects hold no vfuncs), so this is a forward-looking gap vs the gold standard, not an active bug — and whether extend-not-replace subclassing is in gtkx's scope is a genuine product decision, though glib/gtk4 consider chain-up fundamental.

### Object lifetime & threading

**[MEDIUM] Toggle-notify blocks the single GLib main-loop thread on a synchronous Node round-trip whose result is used only for error logging**
- **Where:** `handle/wrapper.rs:88-98` (`invoke_ref_op`) called from `apply_wrapper_level:74` on every strong/weak transition (on-thread `:196` and off-thread `:211`); the block is `node_mailbox.rs` `apply_wrapper_ref_op_and_wait:129-140` → `invoke_node_task_and_wait:142-152` → `wait_for_node_result:164-189`. Result consumed only at `wrapper.rs:93-97` (`Err` → report).
- **Standard:** glib's cross-context handoff is fire-and-forget — `MainContext::invoke` (`main_context.rs:42`, delegating to `:57`) schedules the closure and returns immediately, never blocking for a result. Native already ships the pattern: `schedule_js_reference_delete` (`node_mailbox.rs:66-68`) pushes an env-bound napi_ref delete into the *same* `node_inbox` fire-and-forget.
- **Why it matters:** strong/weak transitions fire whenever a GObject's refcount crosses 1 — essentially every parenting/unparenting during a React commit, many objects — and each stalls the most-contended thread (owner of every libffi/GTK call) for a complete Node round-trip just to synchronously surface an error that could be reported asynchronously. FIFO on `node_inbox` (`push_back`/`pop_front`) already preserves ref/unref ordering and ordering against a later `DeleteReference` regardless of whether the GLib thread waited, so the block buys nothing for ordering.
- **Suggested change:** make the `Unref` (weak) transition fire-and-forget like `DeleteReference`, routing any failure through `ErrorReporter` on the Node side, eliminating the per-transition GLib-thread stall on the React-commit hot path.
- **Constraint fit:** survives — keeps napi work on the Node thread and GTK work on the GLib thread; the hop itself is genuinely forced (napi refcounting is env-bound), only its blocking nature is not. Caveat the finding omits: the synchronous round-trip also guarantees the `Ref` (strong) transition's `napi_reference_ref` lands before the Node thread resumes arbitrary JS/GC, a liveness guarantee (orthogonal to the FIFO ordering argument) closing a narrow window where a still-weak wrapper could be collected while C refs exist. Keep the `Ref`/strong direction synchronous, or prove that window unreachable, before deferring it; the `Unref`/weak direction is unambiguously safe to defer.

**[LOW] Async one-shot self-free arming is separated from construction — a two-phase init glib seals at the `Box::into_raw` site**
- **Where:** `ffi/codec/callback.rs:70-76` (`encode` arms `oneshot_state_ptr` from the caller), `CallbackState::boxed:118-128`, swap in `handle_call:191-198`, `idle_add_local_once` reclaim `:311-321`; `ffi/stashed_value.rs:66-70` (`disarm` does `Box::into_raw`).
- **Standard:** glib registers a closure's allocation and its reclaim hook together at construction with a single owner — `closure.rs:284` `let ptr = Box::into_raw(callback)` paired with `finalize::<F>`'s `Box::from_raw(notify_data)` (`:270`), wired once via `g_closure_add_finalize_notifier(closure, ptr, Some(finalize::<F>))` (`:287`). The self-pointer the reclaim needs is established where the box is created, never handed in by a caller.
- **Why it matters:** native instead arms the `CallbackState` self-pointer in `encode()` (`callback.rs:70-76`), separate from the real construction point `boxed()`; a future edit to the Async encode branch or to `disarm` must be reasoned about across two call frames in two files.
- **Suggested change:** move the `is_oneshot` arming into `boxed()` (using `&*boxed` as the stable heap address), deleting the `encode()` special case. This is a cohesion/SRP cleanup, not a bug fix — the identity `oneshot_state_ptr == the later-leaked box` is already structurally guaranteed by Rust `Box` move semantics (moves never reallocate), so there is no live leak or double-free. The finding's "(or `new`)" alternative is unsound (`new()` returns unboxed `Self`, a soon-dangling stack address); `boxed()` is the only correct target.
- **Constraint fit:** survives — the deferred idle free and the self-reclaim mechanism are forced (a libffi `Closure` cannot free itself mid-trampoline, and async callbacks carry no `GDestroyNotify`), so only the arming site moves; nothing touches dynamic dispatch or the single-thread model.

### Codegen contract

**[MEDIUM] `Descriptor`→`Codec` lowering is memoized for the call path but re-run on every struct-field access**
- **Where:** cached path `bind.rs:30-41` (`into_codec` once into `Arc<CallDescriptor>`, reused via `External` at `call.rs:137`, further memoized by `cached_cif` on `descriptor.id`); uncached path `read.rs:44` and `write.rs:43` both call `field_descriptor.into_codec()?` on every invocation. Codegen emits field accessors inline as `read(getHandle(this), <t.* expr>, offset)` with a compile-time-constant offset (`record-field-accessor.ts:148/177/303/323`, `record-constructor.ts:77`).
- **Standard:** gtk-rs resolves all marshalling at compile time — there is no per-access "parse the type" step; the accessor is monomorphized once over the `translate` traits (`ToGlibPtr` `translate.rs:430`, `FromGlibPtrNone:1555`, `FromGlibPtrFull:1579`). Native's call path deliberately emulates this "resolve once" model via `bind`→`call` with a cached `Codec` (commit c1ad4240, "native call perf"); the field path does not, even though `read`/`write` already separate the reusable `Descriptor` from the per-call `offset`.
- **Why it matters:** two consumers of the same codegen contract have diverged without a reason in the code. For scalar fields the avoidable per-access cost is the N-API descriptor-object marshalling (`Descriptor::from_napi_value`); for compound fields (`t.boxed`/`t.struct`/`t.object`/`t.array`) each access also re-allocates a fresh JS descriptor object and rebuilds a recursive `Codec` `Box`, and the struct-array accessor re-lowers inside a per-element loop (`O(elements×fields)`).
- **Suggested change:** mirror the call path's `bind` pattern — add a bind-style export that lowers a field `Descriptor` to `External<Arc<Codec>>` once, hoist each field's invariant descriptor to a module-level bound handle in codegen, and have `read`/`write` take that handle plus the runtime offset. The JS bind infrastructure already exists (`bind`/`nativeBind`/`createBindCache`).
- **Constraint fit:** survives — wire vocabulary unchanged, lowering stays on the Node thread, codec consumed only on the GLib thread. Tempered: every field access already pays a synchronous cross-thread round-trip (`invoke_glib_and_wait_napi`) that dominates cost, so the durable justification is architectural consistency plus eliminating repeated compound-field JS allocation + descriptor parse, most impactfully in the struct-array loop.

### DRY (semantic)

**[MEDIUM] The "pointer-like codec" category `{Object, Boxed, Struct, Fundamental}` is open-coded at four classifier sites with no shared predicate; three use catch-alls that silently mishandle a new variant**
- **Where:** `array/mod.rs:228` (`ItemCodec::resolve`, the only exhaustive/compiler-checked site), `hashtable.rs:26` (catch-all `_ => None` at `:32`), `ref.rs:51` (`encode`, catch-all `_ =>` scalar-out), `ref.rs:147` (`read`, `_ => bail!` at `:163`); plus the negative form in `RefCodec::supports_inner:28-36`.
- **Standard:** glib names the category once — `unsafe trait TransparentPtrType` (`translate.rs:2762`) — and bounds every container by it (`PtrSlice` `ptr_slice.rs:18`, `List` `list.rs:12`, `SList` `slist.rs:12`); a new pointer-like type implements one marker and every container works, compiler-enforced.
- **Why it matters:** adding a 5th pointer-like `Codec` variant compiles clean but is silently dropped by the hashtable classifier (`_ => None`), mis-encoded by `ref` encode (treated as an 8-byte scalar out-param), and rejected at runtime by `ref` read — because only `ItemCodec::resolve` is exhaustive. The catch-alls already mask an asymmetry: `Ref<BigInt>` is accepted by `supports_inner` and encodable via the ref scalar-out arm yet hits `_ => bail!` on read.
- **Suggested change:** add one exhaustive `Codec::is_handle_backed(&self) -> bool`, route all four sites through it, and reframe `supports_inner` positively so it participates in the same exhaustiveness; the single compile error surfaces at the predicate and correct behavior propagates to all four consumers. Drop the `PointerKind` granularity — no consumer at these sites uses *which* pointer kind.
- **Constraint fit:** fully survives — a shared predicate over the fixed codegen-emitted `Codec` enum is internal Rust, orthogonal to runtime descriptor dispatch and the single-thread model.

### Dead code

**[LOW] `AnchoredValue::Sendable` is a provably-dead enum arm; the enum plus its hand-rolled `unsafe impl Send` can collapse to a single `ThreadGuard` case**
- **Where:** `handle.rs:16-47` (`AnchoredValue` enum, `new`, `droppable_on_current_thread`), `:21` (`unsafe impl Send`), `:72`/`:86` (the only two `AnchoredValue::new` call sites — `Handle::from` and `Handle::clone`).
- **Standard:** measurable dead branch. `new()` picks `Sendable` only when `on_foreign_thread = Mailbox::is_initialized() && !MainContext::default().is_owner()`, but both construction sites are GLib-thread-only: owning `Boxed`/`Fundamental` are built exclusively in codec decode inside `execute()` on the gtkx-glib thread that owns `MainContext::default()`, and `Handle::clone` calls `get_ref()` first, which panics off the owner thread (`glib` `thread_guard.rs:55-57`). So `on_foreign_thread` is always false and `Sendable` is never constructed. `ThreadGuard` is already `unsafe impl Send/Sync` (`thread_guard.rs:123-124`), so the bare-`Value` arm is the sole reason for the hand-written `unsafe impl Send` over an `Rc`-holding `!Send` payload.
- **Why it matters:** two parallel thread-affinity mechanisms encode one invariant, and the dead arm carries a hand-rolled `unsafe impl Send`.
- **Suggested change:** replace `owned_value` with `Option<ThreadGuard<Value>>`, making `Handle` auto-`Send` and letting `handle.rs:21`'s `unsafe impl` be deleted, with `Drop` routing via `guard.is_owner()` → drop else `idle_add_once` exactly as today.
- **Constraint fit:** survives — `ThreadGuard` is a glib primitive, not a static-binding pattern, and the collapse reinforces the single-GLib-thread rule. Corrected motive: contrary to the finding's "latent unsound path" framing, `Sendable`'s `droppable_on_current_thread` uses `MainContext::default().is_owner()` and correctly bounces the free onto the GLib thread, so it is actually the defensive-correct arm; removing it is a dead-code/simplicity cleanup (~10 lines + one `unsafe impl`) that slightly reduces robustness to a hypothetical future off-thread owning-construction refactor, not a safety fix — hence LOW.

## Lower-confidence findings

Each survived one verifier but was contested by another; the actionable core has been narrowed to what holds, and the uncertainty is stated.

- **[MEDIUM, module-layering] `messaging` fuses the two-thread mailbox with a process-global diagnostics facility.** `error_reporter`/`panic_handler`/`log_handler` have zero mailbox coupling, yet three diagnostics-only codec edges (`boxed.rs:6`, `prelude.rs:5`, `enum_flags.rs:42`) reach up into the whole thread-handoff module just to report an error; glib keeps `error` (`lib.rs:117`) and `log` (`:206`) as sibling leaves separate from the threading cluster. Narrowed core: extract diagnostics — together with the generic `build_weak_tsfn` helper it depends on — into a sibling `diagnostics` leaf, redirecting those three edges. *Uncertainty:* `error_reporter` is not import-free (must carry `build_weak_tsfn`), the `handle/wrapper`→`messaging` edge is genuine mailbox traffic and stays, and `messaging` still names `ffi::value` + `handle::wrapper`, so no cycle is fully broken; one verifier rejects the framing and only a narrowed 3-edge redirect survives, downgrading HIGH to MEDIUM/LOW.

- **[MEDIUM, marshalling] Transfer-full Sized/Fixed array return frees its container in the caller, not the decode.** `decode_sized_array` (`array/mod.rs:787`) is the only container decode that does not self-free by ownership; the free is replicated at `call.rs:99-108` and `ref.rs:193-198`, whereas glib puts the container-free inside the decode (`translate.rs:2102-2110`). Narrowed core: DRY only the shared predicate+action into a helper invoked by the two callee-ownership call-sites — do *not* move the free into `decode_sized_array`, which is shared with the `Ref` non-`PtrSlot` path where the pointer is a native-owned Rust `Vec` and an ownership-keyed free would double-free. *Uncertainty:* one verifier reads glib's `from_glib_none_num` as itself free-less (supporting native's current split) and judges the two frees to guard genuinely different provenances (callee-owned vs Stash-owned), i.e. a justified asymmetry rather than a defect.

- **[MEDIUM, dry] Primitive pointer-free operations are triplicated across `PendingRelease`, `BoxedDestructor`, and `Fundamental`.** `g_boxed_free`/`g_free`/extern-unref are re-typed across `stash.rs`, `handle/boxed.rs`, and `handle/fundamental.rs`, and `BoxedFreeFn`/`UnrefFn` are byte-identical aliases, where glib gives each wrapper one `Drop` with one free primitive (`object.rs:399`, `boxed.rs:560`, `shared.rs:475`). Narrowed core: collapse the two aliases into one and extract the shared free primitives into a single `unsafe` helper — but do *not* merge the enums (`GBoxedFreeByName` owns a `GString`, breaking `PendingRelease`'s `derive(Copy)` relied on at `array/mod.rs:478`, and call-scoped vs handle-lifetime frees differ). *Uncertainty:* one verifier rejects as false abstraction — glib parameterizes the free by a compile-time memory-manager type and deliberately does not centralize across container types, and the same-named GList free differs by role (`g_list_free` spine vs `g_list_free_full`).

- **[LOW, marshalling] Container-free-per-transfer-mode is scattered across six array decode paths.** The `if ownership.is_full() { <free> }` gate plus the `storage_owns` double-free guard are re-derived across six sites (`mod.rs:700/718/738/762`, `garray.rs:187/207`), and the guard is present in only two; glib centralizes per container type via `FromGlibContainer` (`slice.rs:479/497/525`). Narrowed core: a single `release_decoded_container(ownership, storage_owns, free_fn)` helper. *Uncertainty:* `decode_ptr_iter` (`mod.rs:660`) already provides this closure seam for three of six kinds, and one verifier holds the guard's locality (only where an inout round-trip happens) is correct, since a shared signature would force the four return-only paths to pass a meaningless `storage_owns`.

- **[LOW, marshalling] Two parallel deferred-transfer carriers duplicate the same conditional-ownership transaction.** `Stash.pending_transfer` (`Vec<PendingTransfer>`, `release_now`) and `CallbackValue.pending_transfer` (`Box<CallbackState>`, `Box::into_raw`) implement the same arm/disarm/release shape with divergent internals; `StashedValue::disarm_pending_transfer` already treats them as one concept. Narrowed core: add `PendingRelease::CallbackStateDrop` and route both through the shared mechanism. *Uncertainty:* this transaction has no glib analogue (eager `IntoGlibPtr` cannot roll back), the shape is already unified via a shared method name + one orchestration loop, and one verifier rejects the merge as downgrading an RAII `Box` to a hand-reconstituted raw pointer while ignoring `CallbackValue`'s extra Call-scope mode.

- **[LOW, subclass] Hand-rolled zeroed `GTypeInfo` + `u16` size casts instead of `g_type_register_static_simple`.** `register_type` (`register_class.rs:247-268`) builds a zeroed `GTypeInfo` and casts sizes to `u16` (`:292-293`) while validation compares against the `u32` width; glib uses `g_type_register_static_simple` with `u32` sizes and `None` init fns (`subclass/types.rs:1077-1085`). Narrowed core: replace with the simple wrapper (`None` init fns are correct — a JS-backed subclass adds no Rust private struct), deleting ~12 lines and the width asymmetry. *Uncertainty:* the "heap corruption" motive is disputed — `GTypeInfo.class_size` is itself `guint16` (`gobject-sys` `lib.rs:667`), so no parent can exceed 65535 and the truncation is provably lossless; the simple wrapper still clamps to `G_MAXUINT16` (returning 0, caught by the existing `bail!`), so this is boilerplate deletion + a guarded error, which one verifier judges a cosmetic nit and the other a real width mismatch in unsafe offset-writing code.

- **[LOW, subclass] Registration can only override vfuncs of interfaces the parent already implements; it never adds a new interface.** `RawInterface::install` (`register_class.rs:154-165`) `g_type_interface_peek`'s an existing vtable and reports+returns on NULL, and the JS driver enumerates only parent interfaces (`register-class.ts:129`); glib adds every declared interface via `g_type_add_interface_static` (`subclass/types.rs:1098-1105`). Narrowed core: on a null vtable, add the interface first with an `interface_init` trampoline built from the existing closure machinery, and let JS offer new interfaces. *Uncertainty:* this is capability-parity with no borne cost today — the components list/tree system is built entirely on existing GObject-backed `GListModel` implementations and never implements an interface in JS — so one verifier rejects it as speculative given non-trivial registration-ordering churn.

- **[LOW, dry] Two hand-rolled reentrant wait loops duplicate the same pump-poll-block RPC primitive.** `wait_for_glib_result` (`node_mailbox.rs:94-107`) and `wait_for_node_result` (`:164-189`) both open-code drain-peer-inbox / poll-result / block-on-`WaitSignal`, the mailbox's most correctness-sensitive invariant asserted twice. Narrowed core: extract one pump-poll-block helper generic over result and error type. *Uncertainty:* the glib `ThreadHandle`/`join` grounding is weak (a plain `rx.recv()` with no pump/reentrancy), `freeze.rs` `run_loop` does not fit (no result channel), and the two loops already diverge in error type, disconnect handling, and poll order — so the shared core is ~4 lines, which one verifier rejects as a false abstraction that would drop the node side's poll-before-drain.

- **[LOW, dead-code] The `Descriptor` wire enum declares three fields the native runtime discards (`Boxed.size`, `Fundamental.type_name`, `Ref.inout`).** All three are declared in the native enum (`descriptor.rs:71/83/113`) but discarded in `into_codec` and stored in no codec; their real consumers are all JS-side. Narrowed core: delete only the three field declarations and their `_` discards from the Rust enum, keeping the codegen/ffi emission (napi ignores the still-emitted JS keys). *Uncertainty:* heavily contested — `Fundamental.typeName` has a *live* JS reader (`gvalue.ts:268` `resolveFundamentalGtype`), and `index.d.ts` is auto-generated from the Rust enum, so deleting the Rust fields removes them from the derived TS types that `boxedT`/`refT`/`callback.ts` assign and read, breaking the TS build; one verifier rejects the finding entirely, so only a careful native-only tidy that provably preserves the JS surface could survive.

## Prioritized roadmap

1. **Wrap the two GTK→Rust trampolines (and the idle cleanup closure) in `catch_unwind` and route to `ErrorReporter`** (Pattern Alignment, HIGH). Highest impact — it converts a class of whole-app aborts on the reverse-callback path into reported, recoverable errors — at trivial churn; do it first.
2. **Return the two codec sites' failures synchronously via `anyhow::Result`** (Pattern Alignment, MEDIUM). Makes invalid-enum and gtype-resolution failures catchable, removes the boxed ownership leak + spurious success, and reuses existing signatures; low effort and correctness-bearing.
3. **Add `Codec::is_handle_backed` and route the four pointer-like classifiers through it** (DRY, MEDIUM). Closes a latent silent-mishandling footgun with one predicate + three arm edits, and gives the boxed error path from step 2 a single membership definition to lean on.
4. **Relocate the five `ffi`↔`handle` back-edges and move `WrapperRefOp` into `messaging`** (Pattern Alignment, MEDIUM + LOW). Pure dependency-direction reorganization; do the two cycle-breaks together since they are the same kind of fix.
5. **Collapse `AnchoredValue` to a single `ThreadGuard<Value>` case and delete the hand-rolled `unsafe impl Send`** (Dead code, LOW). Small self-contained cleanup removing a dead branch and one hand-written `unsafe`.
6. **Move the async one-shot arming into `boxed()`** (Object lifetime & threading, LOW). ~6-line cohesion cleanup co-locating allocation with self-pointer registration, glib-style.
7. **Make the `Unref` (weak) toggle-notify transition fire-and-forget** (Object lifetime & threading, MEDIUM). Removes a per-transition stall of the most-contended thread on the React-commit hot path; sequenced after the cheap cleanups because it requires first confirming the `Ref`/strong GC-liveness window, which stays synchronous.
8. **Memoize field `Descriptor`→`Codec` via a bind-style export in codegen** (Codegen contract, MEDIUM). Restores consistency between the two consumers of the codegen contract and removes compound-field re-allocation/re-lowering; largest churn (native + codegen), so last among the confirmed fixes.
9. **Add vfunc chain-up (stash the parent slot / `parent_class` pointer and expose a "call super" path)** (Subclassing & callbacks, MEDIUM). A real capability gap vs the gold standard with no current consumer, so schedule it after the definite fixes and gate it on the product decision about extend-not-replace subclassing.

The lower-confidence items are optional follow-ups, each pending the decision noted in its uncertainty statement.

*Considered and dropped:* naming `library_cache.rs` after its subordinate `LibraryCache` field rather than the aggregate `GlibThreadState` — the file is already a dedicated sibling leaf (not folded into any `translate.rs` analog), the concept is `pub` and grep-able, and the only proposed change is a filename rename argued from a generic naming principle with a contrived glib analogy, so it does not clear the grounding/cost bar.
